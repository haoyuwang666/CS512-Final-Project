\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

\paragraph{Playback and segment availability.}
Shared repair draws upload capacity from the same Peer that serves
viewers. Under high demand, when the slowest Peer failed, all 6 viewers
finished and shared repair added 3.46\,s of stall per viewer relative to
OFF. The benefit of restoring a copy depended on which holders remained
reachable. When both original
holders stopped after verified repair, the replacement served fresh
requests. Session completion alone therefore gives little indication of
how well the system can serve reads after another holder fails.

\paragraph{Repair timing and bandwidth.}
The grace period determines how long a segment can remain below the
replication target before the coordinator creates a task. Waiting avoided
copying during the brief outages, while longer grace increased one-copy
exposure during persistent outages. Once a task starts copying, the
source allowance limits the upload capacity available to repair.
Compared with F50, L reduced stall when the slowest Peer failed under
simultaneous demand, but the replacement copies took longer to verify.
Across arrival patterns, L showed no consistent advantage in playback
and recovery. The Controller trials also showed that a new leader could
resume a committed task after a crash and register the replacement copy.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Each failed upload capacity in E1--E3 corresponds to a different Peer
and a single paired context. The comparisons therefore include both
Peer identity and capacity, with no estimate of variation across repeats
at each capacity. The repeated trials at 34\,s in E4 illustrate how
delivery outcomes can vary at the same fault interval. All processes
share one host and its OS page cache. Configured upload limits control
contention, and the media clock simulates consumption. Measurements on
wide-area links or with browser decoding could yield different stalls.
Placement, source selection, and retry limits also influence completion.
The failure model covers process crashes with retained disks. Power loss
and sustained network partitions remain outside its scope.

\paragraph{Implications for further experiments.}
To measure when repair improves playback completion, a follow-up could
compare OFF with enabled repair under matched viewer arrival schedules and stop
a second holder before new viewers arrive. Recording the source of each
verified segment would connect a completed session to the replacement
that served it. In the current experiments, verified repair preserved
access after a second holder failure, while its cost to ongoing playback
depended on demand and the upload capacity left at surviving Peers.
