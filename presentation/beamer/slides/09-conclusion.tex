\section{Conclusions}
\begin{frame}{Conclusions: performance and recovery are separate}
\begin{block}{Repair and playback share bandwidth}
Grace avoids some brief-outage work but extends reduced-redundancy exposure.
L showed no consistent advantage over F50.
\end{block}
\begin{block}{Recovery must be checked at the application level}
Controller recovery and task continuity held in tested cases.
E4 exposed availability limits while repair was unfinished.
\end{block}
\small\textbf{Limits:} one host, few contexts, simulated consumption, short horizon.\\[2mm]
\textbf{Next:} multi-host browser measurements; E4 after verified repair completion.
\takeaway{We observed tradeoffs and recovery boundaries within the tested scope.}
\note{To conclude, repair competes with playback for bandwidth. Waiting can avoid unnecessary repair, but extends the time with reduced redundancy. L shows a local tradeoff, without a consistent advantage over F50.

Controller recovery and task continuity hold in our tested cases, while E4 shows the risk of repair still being unfinished.

These are short, single-host experiments with simulated consumption. Next steps are multi-host browser measurements and an E4 control after verified repair completion. Thank you.
No universal adaptive-policy superiority or hard availability guarantee is established.}
\end{frame}
