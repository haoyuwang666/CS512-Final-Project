\section{E5: Controller recovery}
\begin{frame}{E5a and E5b: application recovery after Leader loss}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{Yll}
\toprule Case & Passed & Median recovery \\\midrule
E5a: Leader stops during delivery & 3/3 & 1.52 s\\
E5b: Leader stops with repair pending & 3/3 & 1.60 s\\\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\vspace{3mm}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{E5a:} fresh metadata succeeds; warm and cold sessions complete.
\item \textbf{E5b:} same task ID and creation time; replacement verified and registered.
\end{itemize}
\small Both recovery timers start at Leader stop.
\takeaway{Recovery requires a successful application request, beyond election.}
\note{For E5, we require a successful application request, not just a new Leader.

In E5a, we stop the Leader during delivery. Fresh metadata requests recover, and both warm and cold sessions complete in all three trials. Median recovery is one point five two seconds.

In E5b, we stop the Leader with a committed repair task pending. The new Leader preserves the task ID and creation time, and the replacement is verified and registered. All three trials pass, with median recovery of one point six seconds.
Recovery ranges: E5a 1.47--2.74 s; E5b 1.52--1.97 s.
E5b also checks task state after Controller restart and L configuration after source restart.}
\end{frame}

\takeaway{Median recovery: 1.04 s from quorum restoration; range 0.85--1.43 s.}
\note{E5c removes the Controller majority. During stable majority loss, all nine valid write attempts return HTTP five-oh-three, with no success acknowledgments.

After quorum restoration, the baseline state is retained and the original operation IDs are reconciled. All three trials pass, with median recovery of one point zero four seconds. This timer starts when quorum is restored, excluding the deliberate outage.
The nine attempts span three trials. Coverage is process crashes and quorum loss,
not sustained network partitions, power loss, or Byzantine faults.}
